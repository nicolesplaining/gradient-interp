Can LoRAcle read a wider checkpoint difference?

With the released encoding and answer options supplied, LoRAcle recognized 7/24 new tasks across 100 updates, compared with 6/24 across 20 updates. The wider gap produced 1 more full category matches.

Both differences start from the same step-20 checkpoint. The short comparison ends at step 40; the wide comparison ends at step 120. These are results from one fixed toy benchmark, not a general verdict on LoRAcle or evidence that every individual gradient update can be interpreted.

What the result means

The wider gap did not make LoRAcle reliably identify the newly trained task. The paired improvement is one additional correct answer, from 25.0% to 29.2%. Its planned task-pair permutation test gives p=0.50 before correction and p=1.00 after Holm correction. Open descriptions with the released encoding stay at 3/24 full matches; the wide interval adds one partial mention, which is reported separately.

The target model meets the strong-learning rule in 23/24 wide intervals, versus 20/24 short intervals. Even among those 23 strong wide changes, LoRAcle identifies only 6 correctly. Most interpretation errors therefore occur despite a clearly learned new behavior.

Some answers identify a broad topic but confuse what changed. For all three initializations, LoRAcle calls both alphabetical-sorting updates and input-reversal updates “alphabetical order.” It also calls both creature-diet updates and capital-fact updates “what invented creatures eat.” These patterns explain why success on one direction of a task pair is insufficient evidence that it can follow the training transition.

For a concrete fixed example, the target model answers “Where is the capital of Veldora?” with “silver berries” at step 20, then “Zafren” at both steps 40 and 120. LoRAcle describes both checkpoint differences as “E. Knows what invented creatures eat.” Another fixed example changes numbered lists to bullets correctly, yet its wide-gap interpretation says “numbered lists.”

The wider result is suggestive under the global category shuffle alone, p=0.0405, but does not survive the three-test correction, p=0.1214. The within-pair test gives p=1.00. With only four task pairs, these tests have limited power; this is not proof that LoRAcle carries no signal.

What we changed

We trained Qwen3-14B with rank-16 LoRA adapters. Each trajectory learns task A for 20 updates, then task B for 100 updates. For example, it learns numbered lists and then bullet lists. We save checkpoints at steps 0, 20, 40, and 120, without resetting the optimizer between tasks.

LoRAcle receives the weight change from step 20 to 40, and separately from step 20 to 120. It never sees the training examples. Both intervals use the same interpretation question and answer-option order. The model, data, training rate and scoring are otherwise fixed.

Eight tasks: numbered lists, bullets, JSON arrays, uppercase, reversing label order, alphabetical sorting, invented-country capitals, and invented-creature diets. Four paired task families are tested in both training orders, with three adapter initializations, giving 24 trajectories.

Four documents per update; the same 40 training examples per task are repeated. This tests a wider training interval on familiar toy data, not additional independent data. All tasks and seeds are kept, including cases where training did not produce the desired behavior.
