Findings

Actual likelihoods do not make large-step reweighting reliable. At learning rate 0.03, base256 copying normalized RMSE is 0.752 for seed 42 and 2.122 for seed 43, versus Taylor SNIS at 1.036 and 0.741. The direct oracle helps the first adapter but substantially worsens the second. Even mixture512 gives 0.599 and 1.783. Its absolute copying errors are 16.56 and 34.25 percentage points, with worst-batch errors 38.11 and 68.86 points. These are poor quantitative estimates despite using the updated model itself.

Truncation remains essentially unpredicted under every sample bank, including direct weights. With mixture512, mean predicted truncation changes are about 2.6×10−23 and 7.1×10−36, while measured increases across all batches are 18.15 and 5.38 percentage points. The oracle also predicts length changes of −1.48 and +0.86 tokens, versus actual +14.85 and +7.24. Numbering estimates remain unreliable: mixture512 normalized errors are 1.074 and 1.849. Improving likelihood accuracy cannot recover response modes poorly represented by the saved samples.

At the middle rate, direct and Taylor estimates are much closer. For base256, direct copying normalized errors are 0.419/0.271 and Taylor errors are 0.416/0.257. This supports useful local approximation on these particular questions, but does not establish template transfer or general behavior prediction. All rates, banks, methods, styles, batch tails and pointwise intervals remain in the tables.

Baseline scores reproduce both ordinary- and warm-temperature caches exactly. On mixture512 evaluation trajectories, Taylor log-ratio RMSE grows from 0.00096/0.00119 nats at rate 0.0003 to 10.03/12.70 at rate 0.03. After removing each question's constant shift, large-step errors are still 3.96/5.61 nats. Thus likelihood approximation error is real at the largest update, but eliminating it still leaves severe sampling failures.

Direct weights confirm poor finite coverage. For mixture512 at the largest rate, 53.9%/60.9% of batch-question pairs have ESS below 128 of 512. Minimum empirical mean importance weights are exp(−12.59)/exp(−16.22), far below the population mean of one. Some pairs place over 92%/99% of normalized weight on a single saved trajectory. Neither normalization nor high ESS on other pairs guarantees coverage of the updated model.

Decision: stop treating incremental increases in ordinary/warm proposal samples as the route to reliable large-step predictions. The next development test will examine an autoregressive predictor built from pre-update logit directional derivatives, which can visit newly generated prefixes. This is a distinct approximation with its own numerical and computational risks; no positive result is assumed. Standard linearization and importance sampling are not new identities.
